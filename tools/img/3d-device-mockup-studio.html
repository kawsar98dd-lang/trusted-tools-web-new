How the WebGL 3D Engine Works — Entirely Offline

Under the hood of 3D Mockup STUDIO MAX is Three.js, one of the world's most powerful open-source WebGL libraries. WebGL (Web Graphics Library) is a JavaScript API that directly communicates with your device's GPU through your browser, enabling real-time 3D rendering without any plugins or installations.

When you load this tool, Three.js initializes a Scene, a PerspectiveCamera, and an antialiased WebGLRenderer — the three foundational pillars of any 3D application. The device models (iPhone, MacBook) are constructed procedurally using BoxGeometry with custom material layers and MeshStandardMaterial, which supports physically-based rendering (PBR). This means light interacts with the device surface the way it would in the real world — reflecting off glossy surfaces and casting subtle shadows.

Most impressively, after the initial page load, this entire 3D engine runs 100% offline. You can disconnect your internet, and the tool continues to function perfectly — rotating devices, mapping textures, adjusting lighting, and exporting 4K images.

Screenshot Texture Mapping: From File to 3D Screen

The magic of this tool lies in how it maps your screenshot onto the device's screen face. When you drop an image file, the browser's native FileReader API reads it as a Data URL — a base64-encoded blob that exists only in memory. This data URL is then fed into THREE.TextureLoader, which decodes it into a GPU-compatible texture.

The device geometry is built so that the "screen" face of the phone or laptop corresponds to a specific UV coordinate region. The texture is applied exclusively to that face using material groups on the BufferGeometry — meaning the body of the device keeps its Claymorphism material while only the screen face receives your uploaded screenshot as a live texture.

The result is a photorealistic illusion: your flat screenshot becomes a glowing screen on a three-dimensional device that you can spin 360° with your mouse or touch. This technique, known as UV texture projection, is the same method used by professional 3D studios in Blender and Cinema4D, brought directly to your browser at zero cost.

Client-Side Privacy: Your Images Never Leave Your Device

In most online mockup generators, you click "Upload," and your screenshot travels to a remote server where it is processed, temporarily stored, and often retained for analytics or AI training. This is a significant privacy risk for designers working on unreleased apps, confidential dashboards, or proprietary software.

3D Mockup STUDIO MAX was architected from the ground up to eliminate this risk entirely. There is no backend server. There is no upload endpoint. There is no network request when you drop your image. Everything — texture loading, 3D rendering, lighting calculations, and the final 4K export — runs exclusively within your browser's JavaScript sandbox and GPU context. Your file system is accessed only through the browser's sandboxed FileReader API, which has no ability to send data anywhere without your explicit action.

This architecture is called a Pure Client-Side Application (CSA), and it represents the gold standard of digital tool design for privacy-conscious professionals.

The 4K Export Engine: How Browser-Native High-Res Works

Exporting a high-resolution image from a WebGL canvas without a server is a technique that leverages a powerful but often overlooked browser capability: the HTMLCanvasElement.toDataURL() method. When called, it encodes the current pixel buffer of the canvas as a base64 PNG or JPEG string, entirely in memory.

The trick to achieving 4K resolution is that Three.js's WebGLRenderer supports dynamic resizing. At the moment you click "Export 4K PNG," the tool executes the following sequence in a single synchronous block: it saves the current display dimensions, resizes the renderer to 3840×2160 pixels, calls renderer.render(scene, camera) to draw one frame at that resolution into the off-screen buffer, calls canvas.toDataURL("image/png", 1.0) to capture that buffer as a lossless PNG data URL, triggers an anchor click to download the file, and finally restores the renderer to its original display dimensions. This entire operation takes less than 500 milliseconds on modern hardware.

The output is a full 4K PNG file with zero compression artifacts and absolutely no watermarks — production-ready for your App Store listings, Dribbble shots, Behance portfolios, or marketing landing pages.
