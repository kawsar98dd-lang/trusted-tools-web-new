    Designer's Guide: Creating Scannable Custom QRs

    Making a QR code look good is easy, but making it scan instantly requires following some core design rules. Here is how to use our "Pro Features" effectively:

    1. The Importance of Contrast

    Scanners need contrast to distinguish the data modules from the background. Always ensure your "Primary Color" is significantly darker than your background.
    ✅ Good: Dark Blue on White, Black on Yellow.
    ❌ Bad: Light Grey on White, Red on Pink.

    2. Error Correction Levels (Explained)

    You will see a dropdown labeled "Error Correction". This creates a backup of the data within the QR pattern.

    • Use 'Low (7%)' for simple text or links without logos. It produces the cleanest look.
    • Use 'High (30%)' if you plan to add a Logo in the middle or if the QR will be placed outdoors. This allows the code to work even if 30% of it is covered or damaged.

    3. Logo Placement

    When you upload a logo, our algorithm automatically centers it. We recommend using a square image with a transparent background (PNG). Keep the logo size reasonable; if it covers the "Position Markers" (the three big squares in the corners), the code will fail.

    Many paid services offer "Dynamic QR Codes" that act as a middleman redirect. If their server goes down or you stop paying their subscription, your QR code stops working. This is a huge risk for printed materials.

    Our Solution: We generate 100% Static Codes. The information (URL, Text, WiFi) is directly encoded into the pattern of the image. This means your code will work for 10 years, 50 years, or forever—completely free, with no dependency on our servers.

    Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

    Q: Is there a scan limit?

    No. Since these are static codes, you can scan them a billion times. They rely on the data inside the pattern, not an external server counter.

    Q: Why isn't my QR code working?

    The most common reason is data density. If you paste a very long URL or a massive paragraph of text, the QR dots become too small for phone cameras to focus on. Try using a URL shortener before pasting the link here, or increase the Resolution to "Ultra (1000px)".

    Q: Can I print these on large banners?

    Absolutely. Select the "Max (2000px)" size option. This creates a high-definition PNG file suitable for large format printing without pixelation.
