The Trap of "Double Discounts"

You often see signs like "50% + 20% OFF". It sounds like a flat 70% discount, right? Wrong. Retailers calculate the first 50% off the original price, and then the next 20% off the new reduced price.

For a $100 item, the first discount drops it to $50. The second 20% discount is taken from that $50 (which is $10), making the final price $40. The actual total discount is 60%, not 70%. Our upgraded Smart Discount Calculator now has a dedicated Extra Discount field to handle these cases accurately.

Margin vs. Markup: Do You Know the Difference?

This is where many new businesses lose money. Markup is the percentage you add to your cost to get a selling price. Margin is the percentage of the selling price that is profit.

If you buy a pen for $100 and sell it for $150, your Markup is 50%. However, your Margin is only 33.33%. Our new Markup vs Margin Tool lets you enter a cost price and your desired margin percentage — it instantly tells you what selling price to charge, what the markup is, and what profit you'll make.

The Magic "Rule of 72"

Want a quick mental math trick? To find out how many years it will take to double your money at a specific interest rate, simply divide 72 by that rate.

For example, if you get a 6% return: 72 ÷ 6 = 12 years. While this mental trick is great for estimation, our Compound Interest Calculator gives you the exact down-to-the-cent projection for serious financial planning — with flexible compounding options.

Tipping: Pre-Tax or Post-Tax?

A common debate in restaurants is whether to tip on the bill before tax or after tax. Etiquette experts suggest tipping on the pre-tax amount, as tax is a government fee, not a service provided by the waiter.

However, many card machines suggest tips based on the total (tax-included) amount. Use our Tip Calculator to enter the base bill amount and ensure you are tipping fairly without overpaying on taxes.

Understanding "Negative Growth" (Inflation)

We often use percentage calculators to see how much our investments grew. But have you considered inflation? If your bank gives you 5% interest but inflation is at 6%, your purchasing power has actually decreased by 1%.

You can use the Growth/Decline Calculator to compare your income rise against the inflation rate to see if you are actually getting richer or just maintaining the status quo.

The 50% Loss Trap

If your stock portfolio drops by 50%, how much does it need to grow to get back to even? Most people think 50%. Incorrect.

If $100 drops to $50 (50% loss), you need that $50 to grow by another $50 to reach $100 again. That is a 100% gain. This asymmetry of percentages is crucial for risk management in trading.

Markup is calculated on cost (what you add to cost). Margin is calculated on selling price (your profit as a % of revenue). Use our Markup vs Margin tool to get both figures simultaneously.

How do I calculate reverse percentage?

Use the Reverse Percentage tool. Enter the known value and the percentage it represents — the tool uses the formula (Value / Percentage) × 100 to find the original whole number.

What does compounding frequency mean?

It's how often interest is calculated and added per year. Yearly = once, Quarterly = 4 times, Monthly = 12 times, Daily = 365 times. More frequent compounding means slightly higher returns.

Does 50% + 20% off equal 70% off?

No. The second discount is applied to the already-reduced price, not the original. On a $100 item: 50% off = $50, then 20% off $50 = $40 final. The effective total discount is 60%, not 70%. Use our Smart Discount tool's Extra Discount field to calculate this accurately.
