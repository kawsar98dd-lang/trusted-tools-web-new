Complete Usage Guide: Offline Video Editing in 4 Steps

1

Upload Your Video

Drag and drop any video file (MP4, MKV, MOV, AVI, WEBM) into the upload zone, or click to browse. Files up to 2GB are supported. The video is loaded entirely into your browser's memory — nothing is ever sent to a server.

2

Choose Your Action

Select a tab: Compress (reduce file size), Trim (cut a clip), GIF (animate a segment), Audio (rip the soundtrack), or God Mode (speed control, chroma key, rotation).

3

Configure Settings

Fine-tune each action. For compression, adjust the CRF slider — lower values mean better quality but larger files. For Trim, use the video player to find your exact timestamps. For GIF, balance FPS and width for the perfect loop.

4

Process & Download

Click Start Processing. Watch real-time FFmpeg logs in the terminal. A progress bar shows completion percentage. When done, compare file sizes and click Download Result to save your processed file.

Knowledge Base: WebAssembly, FFmpeg & Video Science

What is WebAssembly (WASM)?

WebAssembly is a binary instruction format that lets C/C++ programs run at near-native speed inside a web browser. FFmpeg — the world's most powerful video processing library, written in C — has been compiled to WASM, allowing us to run it entirely in your browser tab. No server. No cloud. Just raw CPU power from your own machine.

Why Client-Side Protects Your Privacy

Traditional online video tools upload your file to their servers to process it. This means your footage is stored on foreign hardware, potentially scanned, and subject to data breaches. With WASM, your video is read by the browser's memory, processed by the browser's CPU, and the output is written back to your browser's memory. Zero network traffic is ever generated for your video data.

Understanding CRF & Video Compression

The Constant Rate Factor (CRF) controls H.264/H.265 quality. It ranges from 0 (lossless) to 51 (worst). Each ±6 change roughly halves or doubles the file size. A CRF of 23 is visually transparent for most content; 28 is the "sweet spot" for web delivery. The encoder automatically allocates more bits to complex scenes and fewer bits to static ones, which is smarter than a fixed bitrate.

The Science of GIF Optimization

GIFs use an indexed 256-color palette, making them inherently larger than modern formats. FFmpeg's palettegen filter generates an optimal per-video color palette, dramatically reducing artifacts. Lowering FPS from 30 to 10 reduces frame count by 66%. Combined with a reduced scale width (e.g., 320px), a well-optimized GIF from a 50MB video clip can be just 2–5MB with excellent quality.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my video uploaded to any server?

Absolutely not. This tool uses FFmpeg compiled to WebAssembly, which runs 100% inside your browser tab. Your video file is read directly from your local disk into your browser's memory. No data packets containing your video are ever sent over the network. You can even use this tool in Airplane Mode with no internet connection (after the initial WASM engine loads).

Why is processing slower than a desktop app?

WebAssembly runs at roughly 60–80% of native speed and is currently limited to a single thread for FFmpeg execution (due to browser security restrictions around SharedArrayBuffer). A dedicated GPU or multi-core encoding app like Handbrake will always be faster. However, for files under 500MB, performance is very acceptable — and the privacy trade-off is worth it. Future WASM multi-threading support will close this gap.

Why is it slower on older phones or laptops?

Video encoding is one of the most CPU-intensive tasks a device can perform. Older hardware with slower single-core performance will take significantly longer. A 1080p video that takes 2 minutes on a modern laptop might take 10–15 minutes on a low-end Android phone. For mobile, we recommend using 480p output and higher CRF values to reduce processing time. Keeping your browser tab as the focused foreground tab also prevents the OS from throttling it.

My browser tab crashed during processing. What happened?

Large video files (500MB+) require substantial RAM — the original file, the FFmpeg virtual filesystem copy, and the output buffer can peak at 3–4× the original file size in memory simultaneously. If your device runs low on RAM, the browser tab will be killed by the OS. Solutions: Use a desktop browser (Chrome is most efficient), close other browser tabs, lower the output resolution, or process smaller file segments using the Trim tool first.

What browsers are supported?

FFmpeg WASM requires SharedArrayBuffer and COEP/COOP security headers. Chrome 90+, Edge 90+, and Firefox 89+ are fully supported. Safari on iOS/macOS has experimental support but may be unstable for large files. We always recommend the latest version of Chrome for the best experience. If you see a "SharedArrayBuffer not supported" error, your browser or server is missing the required security headers.
